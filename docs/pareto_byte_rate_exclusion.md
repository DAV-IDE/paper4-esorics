# Why `z-score / byte-rate` is NOT on the Pareto front

## Question

`figures/pareto_design_space.pdf` shows 12 configurations
(3 baselines + 9 EAL cells). The Pareto front in
`results/table_pareto_manifest.json` contains only **two** points:

- `z/pkt-rate`     — FPR = 0.005352, delay = 0 s
- `z/dport-entr.`  — FPR = 0.000101, delay = 1034 s

The third baseline `z/byte-rate` is present in the plot but not
on the Pareto front. This document explains why.

## Answer — numeric domination

From `tables/real_data.tex` (which is generated by
`scripts/build_table_and_pareto.py` from the same JSONs that
produce the figure):

| Baseline               | FPR   | Delay | Fires | Detected |
|------------------------|-------|-------|-------|----------|
| `z-score / pkt-rate`   | 0.005 | 0 s   | 96    | yes      |
| `z-score / byte-rate`  | 0.006 | 0 s   | 105   | yes      |
| `z-score / dport-entr.`| 0.000 | 1034 s| 21    | yes      |

The Pareto convention used in `scripts/build_table_and_pareto.py`
is *lower-better on both axes* (FPR on x, detection delay on y).
A point $(x_1, y_1)$ dominates $(x_2, y_2)$ if
$x_1 \le x_2$ and $y_1 \le y_2$ with at least one strict inequality.

Applying this to `z/byte-rate` vs `z/pkt-rate`:

- FPR:   0.005 (`pkt-rate`) **<** 0.006 (`byte-rate`) — strictly better
- Delay: 0 s   (`pkt-rate`) **=** 0 s   (`byte-rate`) — equal

Therefore `z/pkt-rate` weakly dominates `z/byte-rate`
(strictly better on FPR, equal on delay), and `z/byte-rate` is
correctly excluded from the Pareto front.

## Why byte-rate loses to pkt-rate on FPR

The two baselines share the same z-score gate
(`scripts/baseline_zscore.py`, k = 3 rule) and the same warmup
window. They differ only in the input feature:

- **pkt-rate**   is bounded by the physical packet arrival rate of
  the interface. Its stable-phase distribution is compact and its
  spikes during attack seconds are large enough to exceed the
  3-sigma gate on almost every attack second — while during
  benign seconds the tail is thin, keeping FPR low.
- **byte-rate**  is dominated by a handful of high-volume benign
  flows in the CIC-IDS2017-aligned proxy (bulk file transfers,
  large TLS records, elephant flows), which produce a heavier-
  tailed stable distribution than pkt-rate. A fixed 3-sigma gate
  therefore fires slightly more often on the benign phase,
  producing FPR = 0.006 vs 0.005.

This is not a defect of byte-rate as a feature; it is a
scenario-specific artefact of the CIC-IDS2017 Friday-DDoS
afternoon subset, where **packet-count spikes have a much
higher signal-to-benign-tail ratio than byte-count spikes**.
On a different scenario (e.g. a slow-and-low exfiltration
attack) the ordering could easily reverse.

## Why byte-rate is still shown in the figure

Removing byte-rate from the plot would be dishonest: reviewers
would see two z-score baselines and wonder if we hand-picked the
two that make our EAL story cleanest.

Keeping byte-rate visible-but-dominated communicates three things
that are core to the paper's thesis:

1. **The design space contains dominated points.** This is the
   normal case, not a scandal. The Pareto figure earns its name
   precisely because most configurations are *not* on the front.
2. **The choice of feature matters as much as the choice of
   estimator.** A single-feature baseline like z-score is already
   sensitive to feature choice; the same is true for the EAL, and
   this motivates the "configurability" contribution of the paper
   over any fixed detector.
3. **The paper's thesis is auditability, not accuracy.** If we
   quietly deleted the dominated baseline, we would be selecting
   evidence to make the EAL look competitive — which is not the
   claim we make (see the "Three readings of the Pareto figure"
   paragraph in `main_v4.tex`).

## Reproducibility check

To re-verify the exclusion after any re-run:

    python scripts/build_table_and_pareto.py \
        --proxy results/proxy \
        --eal results/eal \
        --baseline results/real \
        --out-tex tables/real_data.tex \
        --out-fig figures/pareto_design_space

Then compare `results/table_pareto_manifest.json` — the
`pareto_front` array must not contain a `"z/byte-rate"` label
under the numbers listed above. If it does, one of the
underlying `baseline_zscore_*.csv` files has drifted, and the
CI byte-rate exclusion needs to be re-examined as an empirical
finding (per the empirical-honesty rule of this repository).
