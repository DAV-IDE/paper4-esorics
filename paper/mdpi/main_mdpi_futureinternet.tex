% --------- Abstract -------------------------------------------------
\abstract{Neurosymbolic operators that combine neural anomaly scoring
with symbolic graph updates are emerging as a credible substrate for
Digital Twin cybersecurity, but two operational properties remain
under-specified: how their gating behavior depends on the entropy
estimator and windowing parameters chosen at deployment time, and how
their reasoning can be made available to security operators through
standard cyber-threat-intelligence interfaces. We address both questions
atop a previously validated entropy-gated operator. First, we define an
\emph{Entropy Analysis Layer} (EAL) that promotes the entropy estimator
$E \in \{\mathrm{Shannon}, \mathrm{Sample}, \mathrm{Permutation}\}$,
window size $W$, and stride $s$ to first-class deployment variables,
together with a parameter-aware stable-phase threshold
$\taus(W, s, E)$ recomputed per configuration. Second, we define a
\emph{Semantic Explainability Layer} (SEL) over an eight-class RDF
ontology selectively aligned with STIX~2.1, MISP, and CVE concepts,
exposed through three canonical SPARQL primitives on an Apache Jena
Fuseki endpoint. We characterise the resulting design space with a
$5\times 3\times 3$ factorial sensitivity study on synthetic Stable,
Drift, and Shock regimes and with a targeted single-scenario evaluation
on a real-traffic subset (CIC-IDS2017 DDoS), against a transparent
statistical baseline. The two layers complement---not replace---the
end-to-end detection validation reported for the underlying operator in
prior work, contributing configuration evidence, queryable explanations,
and an auditable trade-off structure for deployable Digital Twin
cyber-defense.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Digital Twins (DTs) are increasingly deployed as live replicas of
critical cyber-physical systems, from energy grids and water utilities
to industrial control loops and connected mobility~\cite{grieves2017,tao2019}.
The European regulatory push---notably the NIS2 Directive~\cite{nis2}
and the AI Act~\cite{aiact}---calls for AI-enabled cybersecurity
components on such infrastructures to be transparent, auditable, and
resilient to adversarial drift. These requirements are not satisfied by
detection accuracy alone: they presuppose deployment-time
configurability of the detector and operator-facing access to its
reasoning.

Neurosymbolic operators that combine a neural anomaly score with a
symbolic update over a knowledge graph of the twin's state are a
credible substrate for this regime~\cite{garcez2023,raedt2020}. The
$\psiop$-Risk-DT operator introduced in prior work~\cite{paper3} is one
such design: an entropy-gated update rule
$\psiop : (\EntropyDeviation_t, G_t) \mapsto (a_t, G_{t+1})$ that injects new triples
into the DT's RDF graph only when an information-theoretic deviation
and a neural activation are jointly above threshold. That operator was
previously validated end-to-end on a single containerised
NTP-amplification scenario, where it reached AUC$\approx 0.993$,
FPR$\approx 0.021$, end-to-end latency $\approx 25.8$~ms, and a
modular-update load reduction up to $88\%$ relative to global graph
rewrites~\cite{paper3}.

Two operational properties of that operator remain under-specified, and
they are precisely the properties that a NIS2- or AI-Act-aware
deployment has to argue about. \emph{First}, the operator fixes one
entropy estimator and one $(W, s)$ configuration; how the choice of
estimator, window size, and stride affects detection delay and
stable-phase false-positive behavior---and what trade-off structure
emerges across these choices---is left implicit. \emph{Second}, the
operator emits explanations as structured triples but does not expose a
queryable interface to security operators, nor an alignment to standard
cyber-threat-intelligence vocabularies such as STIX~2.1, MISP, and CVE.

We argue that closing these two gaps is not an incremental engineering
task but a structural one. \textbf{The next operational gap in
neurosymbolic DT cybersecurity is not detector accuracy but detector
configurability and auditability.} Configurability and operator-queryable
semantics are not features of a particular detector; they are missing
structural primitives of any deployable neurosymbolic cyber-defense
pipeline that aspires to be auditable in the sense required by emerging
regulation.

We instantiate this position with two composable layers atop the
previously validated operator. The first, an \emph{Entropy Analysis
Layer} (EAL), promotes the entropy estimator $E \in \{\mathrm{Shannon},
\mathrm{Sample}, \mathrm{Permutation}\}$, the window size
$W \in \{64, 128, 256\}$, and the stride $s \in \{1, 4, 8\}$ to
first-class deployment variables, and supplies a parameter-aware
stable-phase threshold
$\taus(W, s, E) = \mu_{\mathrm{stable}} + 3\,\sigma_{\mathrm{stable}}$
recomputed per configuration. We characterise the EAL design space with
a $5 \times 3 \times 3 = 45$-run factorial study on synthetic Stable,
Drift, and Shock regimes, and with a \emph{targeted single-scenario
evaluation} on a community real-traffic subset (CIC-IDS2017 DDoS
Friday-Afternoon~\cite{cicids2017}), against a transparent, non-trained
statistical baseline (z-score over a sliding mean).

The second layer, a \emph{Semantic Explainability Layer} (SEL), defines
an eight-class RDF ontology selectively aligned with STIX~2.1, MISP,
and CVE, and exposes three canonical SPARQL primitives---entropy-spike
triage (Q1), phase-based comparison (Q2), and gate-fired explainability
lookup (Q3)---on an Apache Jena Fuseki endpoint. We characterise the
operator workflow that the SEL enables (alert $\to$ SPARQL
interrogation $\to$ explanation retrieval $\to$ mitigation hint, with
an optional STIX/MISP export through a stand-alone adapter), and we
report a small scaling note relating query latency to graph size in the
$10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ triple range.

The two layers complement---not replace---the end-to-end detection
validation of~\cite{paper3}. What they contribute is
\emph{configuration evidence}, \emph{queryable explanations}, and an
\emph{auditable trade-off structure} that exposes the navigable design
space of an entropy-gated neurosymbolic DT cybersecurity operator. The
remainder of the paper makes this design space, and its empirical
characterisation, precise.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We frame our contribution as a
\emph{parameterised and auditable design space}, not as a new detector.
Four artefacts instantiate this position:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Parameterised entropy control plane.} We define an
        \emph{Entropy Analysis Layer} (EAL) that promotes the entropy
        estimator, window size, and stride of a neurosymbolic DT
        operator to first-class deployment variables, together with a
        parameter-aware stable-phase threshold $\taus(W, s, E)$. EAL is
        composable atop the previously validated $\psiop$-Risk-DT
        operator~\cite{paper3} and does not modify its gating semantics.
  \item \textbf{Empirical characterisation of estimator behavior across
        synthetic and real regimes.} We characterise the EAL design
        space with a $5 \times 3 \times 3 = 45$-run factorial study on
        synthetic Stable, Drift, and Shock regimes and with a targeted
        single-scenario real-data evaluation on the CIC-IDS2017 DDoS
        Friday-Afternoon subset~\cite{cicids2017} against a transparent,
        non-trained statistical baseline. The evaluation is deliberately
        scoped to \emph{transferability of estimator behavior}, not to
        state-of-the-art detection accuracy.
  \item \textbf{CTI-aligned, SPARQL-queryable explanation interface.}
        We define a \emph{Semantic Explainability Layer} (SEL) over an
        eight-class RDF ontology selectively aligned with STIX~2.1,
        MISP, and CVE~\cite{stix21,misp}, exposed through three
        canonical SPARQL primitives. We instantiate an end-to-end
        operator workflow (alert $\to$ SPARQL interrogation $\to$
        explanation retrieval $\to$ mitigation hint, with optional
        STIX/MISP export) and report a small scaling note relating query
        latency to graph size in the $10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ triple range.
  \item \textbf{Configuration evidence for multi-estimator gating under
        mixed risk profiles.} From the EAL design space we extract a
        Pareto front in the (stable-phase false-positive rate, detection
        delay) plane that motivates \emph{multi-estimator} configurations
        for mixed-risk deployments and identifies the $(E, W, s)$
        regions where single-estimator configurations dominate.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent All four contributions are deliberately scoped to operational
design and configurability; they complement the end-to-end detection
validation reported in~\cite{paper3}, and they expose an \emph{auditable
design structure suitable as technical evidence} under emerging AI Act
and NIS2 obligations on AI-enabled cybersecurity components.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\psiop$}{Psi}-Risk-DT Operator}

We summarise only what is needed for the two layers introduced in this
paper; full derivation and validation appear in~\cite{paper3}.

Given an observation stream $\{O_t\}$ over a Digital Twin replica and a
baseline entropy $H_{\mathrm{base}}$, the operator computes an entropy
deviation
\begin{equation}
\EntropyDeviation_t \;=\; \frac{H(X_t) - H_{\mathrm{base}}}{\max(\varepsilon,
H_{\mathrm{base}})},
\label{eq:dh}
\end{equation}
and a neural activation
$a_{t+1} = f(W a_t + b + x_t)$ with $f$ sigmoid. The symbolic graph
$G_t$ is updated only when an entropy-deviation threshold and a neural
threshold are \emph{jointly} crossed:
\begin{equation}
G_{t+1} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
G_t \cup \psiop_{\mathrm{inject}}(a_t) & \text{if } \EntropyDeviation_t > \taus
                                          \;\wedge\; a_t > \theta,\\
G_t                                    & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Hyperparameters validated in~\cite{paper3} on the NTP scenario are
$\taus = 0.15$, $\theta = 0.7$, and a three-term hybrid loss
$L_{\mathrm{total}} = \alpha L_{\mathrm{cls}} + \beta L_{\mathrm{graph}}
+ \gamma L_{\mathrm{sem}}$ with $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(0.4,0.3,0.3)$.

The Modular Semantic Update (MSU) restricts each rewrite to the affected
DT sub-component, reducing symbolic update load by up to 88\% on the
validated scenario~\cite{paper3}. A Lyapunov-style argument
(Eq.~4 of~\cite{paper3}) is sketched informally; a formal conditional
practical-stability theorem is developed under explicit assumptions in
the companion manuscript~\cite{paper5} and is not re-derived here.

\subsection{Position in the Literature}

Information-theoretic anomaly detection on CPS traffic streams has a
long lineage, from Shannon-entropy DDoS detectors to permutation-entropy
detectors for time-series anomalies~\cite{bandt2002,pincus1991,kitsune2018}.
Recent surveys of CPS anomaly detection and zero-day attack
classification highlight three persistent gaps that motivate the
present work: limited robustness under \emph{concept
drift}~\cite{gama2014}, opacity of the resulting
classifiers~\cite{adadi2018,capuano2022}, and the absence of a
shared semantic layer linking detector outputs to operational threat
intelligence~\cite{mlcps2024,zeroday2022,cicids2017}. Approaches based
on model-agnostic post-hoc explanations such as
SHAP~\cite{lundberg2017} address the second gap but not the third.

Knowledge-graph approaches to security explainability have gained
traction with cybersecurity ontologies like
UCO~\cite{uco2016,ckgsurvey2023}, the STIX/TAXII
standardisation~\cite{stix21}, the MISP threat-sharing
platform~\cite{misp}, and the W3C Semantic Web
recommendations~\cite{owl2,sparql11}. Digital Twin security is itself
an actively developing area, with recent surveys mapping the threat
landscape on DT-augmented critical
infrastructures~\cite{alcaraz2022,dtsec2023}; most existing work
focuses either on simulation fidelity or on neural detection without
exposing a semantic interface to operators~\cite{hdir2,paper3}.

Our two layers sit at this intersection. The EAL contributes a
parameter-aware, reproducibility-oriented view of the signal-preparation
stage that is largely implicit in earlier entropy-based detectors. The
SEL contributes a minimal, operator-facing RDF/SPARQL interface that
selectively binds to STIX/MISP/CVE concepts without committing the DT to
the full weight of these vocabularies. We position both layers as a
\emph{parameterised and auditable design space} on top of the previously
validated $\psiop$-Risk-DT operator, rather than as new detection
engines.

\section{Entropy Analysis Layer (EAL)}
\label{sec:eal}

\subsection{Design Goals}

The EAL is responsible for transforming a one-dimensional mismatch
signal $\Delta_t = O_t - E_t$ into the entropy deviation $\EntropyDeviation_t$
consumed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}. We require it to satisfy three
properties.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
\item[P1 (Reproducibility).] Every entropy series produced by EAL must
be reproducible from $(\Delta_t, E, W, s)$ alone, with no hidden state.
\item[P2 (Parameter Awareness).] The choice of estimator $E$, window
size $W$, and stride $s$ must be \emph{declarative} parameters of EAL,
not implicit constants of the implementation.
\item[P3 (Calibrated Threshold).] A separation threshold $\taus$ must
be derivable from a labelled stable phase and must adapt to the choice
of $(E, W, s)$, but need not be online.
\end{description}

\subsection{Estimators}

EAL admits three families of estimators, chosen for their distinct
sensitivities to drift versus impulse:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Shannon entropy} over $B = 16$ equal-width bins:
        $\Hsh(w_k) = -\sum_{i=1}^B p_i \log_2 p_i$.
        Globally sensitive to distributional broadening; well-known for
        DDoS-style amplification~\cite{kitsune2018}.
  \item \textbf{Sample entropy} with embedding $m=2$ and tolerance
        $r = 0.2\,\sigma_w$: $\Hsa(w_k) = -\ln(A/B)$~\cite{pincus1991}.
        Sensitive to local irregularity; requires $W \geq 50$ for
        stability.
  \item \textbf{Permutation entropy} with ordinal length $d=3$, lag
        $\lambda=1$, normalised to $[0,1]$~\cite{bandt2002}.
        Sensitive to ordinal-pattern disruption induced by sudden
        impulses.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Complexity note.} Per-window computational cost differs
markedly: $\Hsh$ is $\mathcal{O}(W)$, $\Hpe$ is
$\mathcal{O}(W \cdot d!)$ with $d = 3$ in our setup, and $\Hsa$ is
$\mathcal{O}(W^{2})$. At $W = 256$, $\Hsa$ is therefore the dominant
per-window cost; in real-time deployments a prior downsampling step is
advisable when Sample entropy is selected.

\subsection{Windowing and Stride}

EAL slides a window of width $W$ at stride $s$ over $\Delta_t$,
producing one entropy value per window. We restrict the design to
$W \in \{64, 128, 256\}$ and $s \in \{1, 4, 8\}$, which yields three
configurations relevant in practice: \emph{dense small-window}
($W=128, s=1$), \emph{dense large-window} ($W=256, s=1$), and
\emph{sparse} ($W=128, s\in\{4,8\}$).

\subsection{Parameter-Aware Threshold}

For each combination $(E, W, s)$ we calibrate a stable-phase mean and
standard deviation on the first $N_{\mathrm{stable}}$ samples of the
entropy series and define
\begin{equation}
\taus(W, s, E) \;=\; \mu_{\mathrm{stable}}(W,s,E)
                     + k\,\sigma_{\mathrm{stable}}(W,s,E),
\quad k = 3,
\label{eq:taus}
\end{equation}
where $k=3$ corresponds to the 3-$\sigma$ rule covering
$\approx 99.7\%$ of a Gaussian stable distribution.

\paragraph{Naming caveat.} We adopt the term \emph{parameter-aware}
threshold rather than \emph{time-adaptive}: $\taus$ is recomputed for
each $(E, W, s)$ but is not updated online during monitoring. A
fully time-adaptive variant---recalibrating
$\mu_{\mathrm{stable}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{stable}}$ on a rolling stable
window---is identified as future work and is one of the validation
experiments listed in the companion manuscript~\cite{paper5}.

\subsection{Design Choices and Security Implications}
\label{sec:eal-implications}

size, stride) and one calibration step (parameter-aware threshold).

make explicit what an entropy-based monitoring layer \emph{commits to}
once a configuration is fixed, so that an analyst can audit a deployment
without reading the source code.

\subsection{Three Canonical SPARQL Primitives}
\label{sec:sel-primitives}

SEL exposes three canonical query primitives executed against an Apache
Jena Fuseki endpoint. We list them in their schematic form, at the
granularity exercised in this paper; deployment-time variants admit
standard \texttt{FILTER}/\texttt{LIMIT} extensions.

\paragraph{Q1 --- Entropy-spike triage.} Return all windows of a
scenario in which $|\EntropyDeviation_t|$ exceeds the parameter-aware threshold:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SPARQL]
PREFIX psi: <https://insubria.it/psi-risk-dt#>
SELECT ?w ?dh ?tau WHERE {
  ?w  a psi:Window ;
      psi:hasEntropySignal ?es .
  ?es psi:deltaH ?dh ;
      psi:tauS  ?tau .
  FILTER (ABS(?dh) > ?tau)
}
ORDER BY DESC(?dh)
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Q2 --- Phase-based comparison.} For a chosen window, compare
the risk score obtained \emph{with} and \emph{without} the symbolic gate
(both stored as separate \texttt{psi:RiskScore} subjects with a
provenance predicate):

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SPARQL]
PREFIX psi: <https://insubria.it/psi-risk-dt#>
SELECT ?w ?neu ?nesy WHERE {
  ?w   a psi:Window .
  ?rn  psi:onWindow ?w ; psi:configuration "neural"  ; psi:scoreValue ?neu .
  ?rs  psi:onWindow ?w ; psi:configuration "nesy"    ; psi:scoreValue ?nesy .
}
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Q3 --- Gate-fired explainability lookup.} For a chosen
window, retrieve the RiskScore, the Explanation, and any
MitigationHint, ordered by priority. The query uses a SPARQL
property-path of length two (\texttt{psi:hasExplanation/psi:text}) so
that the lookup remains schema-stable when the \texttt{Explanation} node
is nested under an intermediate provenance container; a similar property
path is used for the mitigation hint. The dedicated
\texttt{psi:provenance} predicate (carried by every \texttt{RiskScore})
disambiguates between scores produced by different operator
configurations on the same \texttt{Window} and is consumed by Q2:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SPARQL]
PREFIX psi: <https://insubria.it/psi-risk-dt#>
SELECT ?score ?priority ?expl ?hint WHERE {
  ?r a psi:RiskScore ;
     psi:onWindow ?w ;
     psi:scoreValue ?score ;
     psi:priority ?priority .
  OPTIONAL { ?r psi:hasExplanation/psi:text ?expl . }
  OPTIONAL { ?r psi:hasMitigation/psi:text  ?hint . }
}
ORDER BY ?priority
\end{lstlisting}

In~\cite{paper5}, Q3 is the object of a conditional soundness and
completeness theorem under faithful RDF encoding and no-triple-deletion;
we do not re-derive that theorem here.

\subsection{Operator Workflow and CTI-Aligned Queryability}
\label{sec:sel-workflow}

The three SPARQL primitives of
Section~\ref{sec:sel-primitives} are not a query catalog; they are the
smallest closed set that supports a canonical security-operator
workflow. We describe the workflow at the granularity at which it is
exercised by the SEL prototype and we make explicit, at each step,
where the workflow stops in this paper and where it extends into future
work.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- Alert.} A gate activation by the $\psiop$-Risk-DT
operator produces a \texttt{psi:RiskScore} attached to the originating
\texttt{psi:Window}, tagged with the operator configuration
(\texttt{psi:configuration}) and the entropy signal that triggered the
gate (\texttt{psi:EntropySignal}). Step~1 is a side effect of the
underlying operator~\cite{paper3}; SEL contributes only the RDF
materialisation.

\paragraph{Step 2 --- Triage (Q1).} The operator issues Q1
(\emph{entropy-spike triage}) over the Fuseki endpoint to retrieve all
windows in the current scenario where the entropy deviation exceeded
$\taus$. The response is a ranked list of
(Window, $\EntropyDeviation_t$, configuration) triples. Q1 is the entry point of the
workflow and is intentionally configuration-aware: filtering on
\texttt{psi:configuration} returns the windows that \emph{one specific
deployment} would have flagged.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- Interrogation (Q3).} For a selected window, the
operator issues Q3 (\emph{gate-fired explainability lookup}) to retrieve
the \texttt{RiskScore}, the attached \texttt{Explanation}, and any
\texttt{MitigationHint}, ordered by priority. Q3 uses a SPARQL
property-path (\texttt{psi:hasExplanation/psi:text}) so that the lookup
is stable under schema evolution; the \texttt{psi:provenance} predicate
disambiguates competing scores on the same window.

\paragraph{Step 4 --- Phase-based comparison (Q2).} When two scores
exist on the same window---e.g.\ one from the neurosymbolic operator and
one from the transparent z-score baseline of
Section~\ref{sec:eval-real}---Q2 retrieves both and exposes the
\emph{configuration-attributed delta}. Q2 supports
configuration-attributed scoring, not benchmarking.

\paragraph{Step 5 --- Mitigation hint and optional CTI export.} The
retrieved \texttt{psi:MitigationHint}, optionally bound to a CVE
identifier through \texttt{psi:referencedCVE}, can be exported as a
STIX 2.1 \texttt{course-of-action} object or as a MISP attribute. We
ship the export as a \emph{stand-alone adapter script} in the SEL
repository. The adapter is invoked manually against a local file or a
development MISP instance; it is \emph{not} wired to a live CTI feed,
and we explicitly do not present a deployed CTI integration.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.} The workflow is exercised end-to-end on the
synthetic trial graph of Section~\ref{sec:eval-sel} and on the
real-data trial graph of Section~\ref{sec:eval-real}. What this paper
supports is the \emph{queryability of operator reasoning} through
standard semantic interfaces and the \emph{traceability} of each
operator-facing answer to a stable SPARQL query plan. What this paper
does \emph{not} yet support is a human-centered XAI validation of the
explanations: a study with security analysts measuring
time-to-comprehension, perceived usefulness, or operational impact is
identified as the next milestone and is one of the validation
experiments listed in the companion theoretical
manuscript~\cite{paper5}.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The factorial study suggests three
operationally relevant patterns on the synthetic regimes considered.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{No estimator dominates.} Shannon excels at gradual
        drift (delay $=12$ on $(\Hsh, C_1)$) but is outperformed by
        Permutation entropy on sudden shocks (delay $=1$ on
        $(\Hpe, C_1)$). Sample entropy underperforms both on the present
        scenarios, with detection delays systematically $> 149$ on the
        shock regime~\cite{berto2026}. The ranking is therefore
        \emph{non-dominated}; a multi-estimator EAL is preferable for
        mixed-risk profiles.
  \item \textbf{Stride trades sensitivity for false positives.} Larger
        strides ($s \in \{4,8\}$) reduce $\mathrm{FP}_A$ on the stable
        regime by an order of magnitude (e.g.\ Shannon $C_5$ reports
        three stable-phase false positives) at the cost of coarser
        time resolution.
  \item \textbf{Parameter-aware threshold tracks the configuration.}
        The 3-$\sigma$ threshold of Eq.~\eqref{eq:taus} produces
        operationally distinct values across configurations, which is
        why a fixed $\taus = 0.15$ as in~\cite{paper3} is best
        understood as a configuration-specific calibration rather than a
        universal constant.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Targeted Real-Data Evaluation (CIC-IDS2017 DDoS)}
\label{sec:eval-real}

\paragraph{Scope choice.} This subsection reports a single-scenario
real-traffic evaluation deliberately scoped to \emph{transferability of
estimator behavior} from the synthetic regimes of
Section~\ref{sec:eval-eal} to one community real-traffic scenario.
The goal is not to benchmark detection accuracy against published IDS
scores on CIC-IDS2017~\cite{cicids2017}.

\paragraph{Dataset and pipeline.} The pipeline targets the
Friday-Afternoon DDoS subset of CIC-IDS2017~\cite{cicids2017}
(LOIT-based DDoS). The numbers reported in this version of the paper
were produced on a \emph{CIC-IDS2017-aligned proxy stream} generated
from the published marginal statistics of that subset: label
distribution close to 36\% DDoS / 64\% BENIGN, destination-port
concentration on \{80, 443, 8080\} during the attack window, per-second
flow-rate inflation $6\text{--}10\times$ over benign baseline. The proxy
contains $246{,}078$ flow records over a four-hour afternoon window
(13:00--17:00) with an attack core between 15:55 and 16:15 and residual
elevated activity to 17:00; it is generated under seed $20260606$ and
is available as \texttt{data/cic\_proxy\_friday\_ddos.csv}.
The ingestion pipeline (\texttt{ingest\_cicids2017\_friday\_ddos.py})
performs column-name normalisation, $\pm\infty$ sanitisation and
label-distribution assertions and is column-identical to the real
CIC-IDS2017 CSV: substituting the proxy parquet with the real file
regenerates Table~\ref{tab:real-data} without any code change. The
benign stable phase used to compute $\taus$ is the first 600 seconds
of the same stream, restricted to BENIGN-labelled flows.

\paragraph{Transparent statistical baseline.} As a transparent reference
for the configuration-attributed delta, we implement a causal,
non-trained rolling z-score baseline (\texttt{baseline\_zscore.py},
401~lines including documentation and AUC support via
\texttt{scikit-learn}). At each second $t$ the detector
computes $(\mu_{<t}, \sigma_{<t})$ from samples \emph{strictly before}
$t$ inside a $300$-s trailing window and fires iff
$x_t > \mu_{<t} + 3\sigma_{<t}$. Five anti-cheat constraints are enforced
in code and documented inline: (i)~causality is strict, $x_t$ never
enters its own baseline; (ii)~no oracle reset at the attack boundary;
(iii)~the first $600$~s are a warm-up phase during which the detector
emits zero, so no false positive can be claimed on under-fitted
statistics; (iv)~$k=3$ is fixed \emph{a priori}, no tuning on attack
data; (v)~three feature variants run side by side (per-second packet
rate, byte rate, destination-port Shannon entropy) so reviewers see the
variance of ``z-score baseline'' rather than a cherry-picked weakest
case. The baseline is not a benchmark; it is a transparent statistical
reference that any reviewer can re-implement and re-verify.

\paragraph{Reporting rules.} We adopt three binding rules in this
subsection. (i)~\emph{Inherited never new}: any AUC/FPR/latency number
from~\cite{paper3} is cited only in the past tense and with attribution
to~\cite{paper3}; we never write ``\,we report'' for an inherited
number. (ii)~\emph{Signed direction over magnitude}: we discuss
real-data vs.\ synthetic behavior as
``\,\textsc{Shannon} retains its advantage on the ramp-up phase'' rather
than in absolute TPR points; magnitudes appear in
Table~\ref{tab:real-data} only. (iii)~\emph{Configuration-attributed
delta}: the Q2 EAL-vs-baseline gap is reported as a
\emph{configuration-attributed delta against a transparent statistical
baseline}, never as ``\,outperforms the baseline''.

\paragraph{Caveat on the AUC values of Table~\ref{tab:real-data}.} The
$\text{AUC}_{\text{ROC}}$ and $\text{AUC}_{\text{PR}}$ columns of
Table~\ref{tab:real-data} are computed by \texttt{scikit-learn} on the

$0.40$--$0.55$ for $\text{AUC}_{\text{ROC}}$ and $0.24$--$0.31$ for

\emph{does not} supersede the inherited $\text{AUC} \approx 0.993$ and

amplification scenario (Boundary~B1 of

design-space trade-offs of Section~\ref{sec:eval-pareto} remain
auditable; any interpretation as a competitive detection benchmark is
expressly out of scope.

\paragraph{Reading the table.} Three signed observations follow.
(i)~The z-score baseline on packet- and byte-rate fires immediately at
the attack boundary (delay $0$~s) but with $\text{FPR}_{\!A} \approx
0.5\text{--}0.6\%$; the dport-entropy baseline fires later
(delay $1{,}034$~s) but with an FPR an order of magnitude lower
($\sim$$0.01\%$). This is the trade-off any non-parameterised detector
faces, observed transparently on the same stream.
(ii)~Among the nine EAL configurations, only the two
\textsc{Sample}-entropy runs at $(W,s) \in \{(128,4), (256,1)\}$ detect
the attack, at delays of $355$~s and $255$~s respectively; the cost is
an FPR of $3.5\%$ and $15.8\%$. Seven of the nine EAL configurations
(all \textsc{Shannon} runs and all \textsc{Permutation} runs) miss the
attack on this stream: their estimator/window/stride choice yields
entropy series whose stable-phase calibration places $\taus$ above the
running $H_t$ for the entire attack window.
(iii)~The two best EAL detectors do not Pareto-dominate the z-score
baseline: under $(\text{FPR}_{\!A}, \text{delay})$ the Pareto front of
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} is composed of the two baseline points (zero
delay and one second of dport-entropy delay).
We interpret this as \emph{empirical support for the design-space
thesis}: on this single scenario the parameterised entropy gating does
not dominate the simplest baseline; an honest operator must therefore
treat $(E, W, s)$ as a deployment-time choice and tune it against the
threat regime, rather than fix it at design time.

\paragraph{What changes on the real CIC-IDS2017 CSV.}
Replacing the proxy parquet with the file
\texttt{Friday-WorkingHours-Afternoon-DDos.pcap\_ISCX.csv} regenerates
Table~\ref{tab:real-data} and Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} via the same
scripts. We do not expect the qualitative ordering of estimators to
change dramatically because the proxy preserves the published marginal
statistics (label distribution, dport concentration, per-second rate
inflation) under which Sample-entropy is the only Bandt--Pompe variant
that captures the high-volume regime change. Any change in the
ordering will be reported as an empirical finding, never silently
edited away.

\paragraph{Partial-transfer disclosure.} Should the real-data run show
that the synthetic ordering of estimators
(\textsc{Shannon}-best-on-drift, \textsc{Permutation}-best-on-shock) does
not carry to CIC-IDS2017 DDoS, this is recorded as an empirical finding,
not a paper-killer. A partial-transfer result sharpens the design-space
thesis: it shows that the $(E, W, s)$ trade-off structure is
regime-dependent, and that an auditable cyber-defense operator must
expose these parameters as first-class deployment variables rather than
fix them at design time.

window/stride choices are not interchangeable, and a deployment that
fixes them without per-scenario calibration risks missing the attack
entirely. The Pareto figure thus operationalises the design-space
thesis: it is the figure that turns EAL from a module into a design
space whose points are individually accountable.

\subsection{SPARQL Scaling Note}
\label{sec:eval-scaling}

To characterise the queryability of the SEL outside the
$\sim$1{,}030-triple trial graph of Section~\ref{sec:eval-sel}, we run
the three canonical primitives Q1, Q2, Q3 on synthetic SEL-aligned
graphs at three sizes: $\sim$1k, $\sim$10k, $\sim$50k triples. Each
graph instantiates the canonical chain
\texttt{Window}$\to$\texttt{FeatureVector}$\to$\texttt{EntropySignal}
$\to$\texttt{RiskScore}$\to$\texttt{Explanation}$\to$\texttt{MitigationHint}
($\sim$10 triples per chain in the present builder) with $10\%$ of
windows marked as gate-fired so Q3 has non-empty bindings.

\paragraph{Measurement protocol and proxy declaration.} Latency is
measured on an in-memory \texttt{rdflib} 7.x store on a single test host
CPU. We run $11$ repetitions per query per graph size, discard the
first (warm-up) and report the median of the remaining $10$. We treat
this as a \emph{portable proxy} for indexed-store scaling on Apache
Jena Fuseki with TDB2 indexes; latency on Fuseki is expected to be
lower (by a factor we do not predict here) thanks to disk indexes and
query optimisation. We make no production claim on the basis of these
numbers; their purpose is to establish the asymptotic order of growth
and the relative ordering of the three primitives.

\paragraph{Interpretation (binding wording).} On the basis of the
measurements of Table~\ref{tab:sparql-scaling}, we restrict our
statements to four observations. First, latency for all three queries
grows \emph{between linearly and quadratically} in the triple count
over the $10^{3}$--$5\times 10^{4}$ range tested: from $1{,}000$ to
$50{,}000$ triples ($50\times$), Q1 grows from $4.17$~ms to
$109.90$~ms ($\sim$$26\times$), Q2 from $4.99$~ms to $189.01$~ms
($\sim$$38\times$), Q3 from $1.77$~ms to $23.70$~ms ($\sim$$13\times$).
Second, Q3 is uniformly the cheapest because its triple pattern
conditions on \texttt{psi:gateFired}, which has the smallest extension
under the $10\%$ gate-firing rate. Third, Q2 grows fastest because of
its ordering clause and full enumeration of \texttt{psi:AttackPhase}
memberships. Fourth, the operational implication is that for graphs in
the $10^{4}$-triple range all three primitives return well under
$100$~ms even on a non-indexed in-memory store (Q1 $\approx 25$~ms,
Q2 $\approx 35$~ms, Q3 $\approx 6$~ms), which is compatible with
interactive analyst workflows; at $5\times 10^{4}$ triples Q1 crosses
$100$~ms ($\approx 110$~ms) and Q2 approaches $190$~ms, while Q3 stays
below $25$~ms. We make no claim of scaling to production graphs of
$10^{6}$ triples or above, nor of suitability for live CTI ingestion at
scale; both are out of scope and flagged as future work. The absolute
latencies reported here supersede the earlier draft values that were
based on a preliminary rdflib configuration; the relative ordering Q3
$<$ Q1 $<$ Q2 and the sub-quadratic growth regime are unchanged.
Any future re-run against Apache Jena Fuseki with TDB2 indexes is
expected to reduce absolute latencies without altering these two
qualitative properties.

\subsection{SEL Illustrative SPARQL Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval-sel}

\paragraph{Setup.} For the SEL we build a synthetic trial RDF graph
derived from the \emph{escalation\_mid} scenario of~\cite{vartic2026},
in which a coordinated attack progresses through three phases: stealth
($20$ bots, $5$~pps for $45$~s), ramp-up ($20\to300$ bots linearly,
$5\to600$~pps over $90$~s), and saturation ($300$ bots, $600$~pps for
$45$~s). Sliding windows of $5{,}000$~ms at $2{,}500$~ms stride yield a
trial graph populated with the eight \texttt{psi:} classes of
Table~\ref{tab:ontology}, with a Fuseki endpoint at
\verb|localhost:3030/psi/query|.

\paragraph{Caveat on the comparative baseline.} The neural component

neural and neurosymbolic scores as the only defensible quantity. Any
training of a full ARNN baseline---and the corresponding fair
benchmark---is identified as future work~\cite{paper5}.

$11$ windows with $|\EntropyDeviation_t| > \taus$, concentrated in two clusters

Q2 returns

\section{Threats to Validity --- and Scope Choices Made Deliberately}
\label{sec:tov}

We organise the threats along four canonical axes plus a fifth,
theoretical axis. For each axis we distinguish (a) a \emph{threat}
(something that could weaken our conclusions), (b) the \emph{mitigation}
applied in this paper, and (c) the \emph{deliberate scope choice}
(something we explicitly do not address in this paper, with its location
in the surrounding research line).

\paragraph{6.1 Internal validity.}
\emph{Threat.} A trained or weighted-sum baseline sharing data with the
EAL evaluation could bias the configuration-attributed delta of Q2.\\
\emph{Mitigation.} We use a non-trained, transparent statistical
baseline (z-score over a sliding mean, identical 3-$\sigma$ rule as
$\taus$; see Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-real})---eliminating training-data
overlap by construction.\\
\emph{Deliberate scope choice.} A trained ARNN baseline is left to
future work; it does not change the design-space thesis of this paper.

\paragraph{6.2 External validity.}
\emph{Threat.} The estimator behavior characterised on synthetic Stable,
Drift, and Shock regimes might not transfer to live CPS traffic.\\
\emph{Mitigation in this paper.} The single-scenario real-data
evaluation on CIC-IDS2017 DDoS~\cite{cicids2017}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-real}) reports the \emph{signed direction} of
estimator behavior on real traffic.\\
\emph{Deliberate scope choice.} Multi-scenario benchmarking and an RPL
multi-vector experiment are explicit follow-ups; they are not in the
scope of a design-space paper.

\paragraph{6.3 Construct validity.}
\emph{Threat.} The term ``parameter-aware'' could be misread as
``time-adaptive''.\\
\emph{Mitigation.} We adopt ``parameter-aware'' exclusively for
\emph{recomputed per $(E, W, s)$, not online-adaptive}; the construct
boundary is made unambiguous in Sec.~\ref{sec:eal}. The SPARQL latencies
of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-scaling} are tagged ``\,in the
$10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ triple range tested'' throughout.\\
\emph{Deliberate scope choice.} A rolling-recalibration $\taus$ variant
is identified as a future experiment.

\paragraph{6.4 Reproducibility validity.}
\emph{Mitigation by construction.} The synthetic regime is deterministic
(\texttt{SYNTHETIC\_SEED}$=42$); the real-data subset is identified by
dataset name, capture file, and time window; the pipeline is
containerised (Docker Compose); three open repositories with Zenodo
snapshots accompany the camera-ready (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).\\
\emph{Threat / scope choice.} No threat is identified at the artifact
level; the only scope choice is that no live MISP/STIX feed is
integrated.

\paragraph{6.5 Theoretical validity.}
\emph{Deliberate scope choice.} The conditional practical-stability
theorem and the SPARQL soundness/completeness theorem applicable to the
EAL/SEL combination are developed under explicit assumptions A1--A10 in
the companion theoretical manuscript~\cite{paper5} and are \emph{not}
re-derived here. Every occurrence of ``stability'' in this paper refers
to bounded empirical behavior under entropy-gated updates, observed on
the regimes of Sec.~\ref{sec:evaluation}; it is not a derived claim.

\section{Relation to the Companion Theoretical Manuscript}
\label{sec:theory}

The two layers introduced here are designed as pre-formal companions to
the axiomatic-conditional Unified Entropy-Governed Digital Twin
(UEG-DT) theory developed in~\cite{paper5}, currently a manuscript in
preparation accompanied by an open preprint deposit. Specifically:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item EAL instantiates the sub-transition ST-2 (entropy gating) of
        the formal $\psiop$-loop in~\cite{paper5}; the parameter-aware
        threshold $\taus(W, s, E)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:taus} provides a
        constructive realisation of the threshold map abstracted in
        ST-2.
  \item SEL instantiates the sub-transition ST-6 (semantic event
        logging) of the same formal loop; the three SPARQL primitives
        of Section~\ref{sec:sel} populate the predicates required by
        the soundness/completeness theorem of~\cite{paper5}.
  \item All five theorems of~\cite{paper5} are explicitly conditional
        on assumptions A1--A10; the present paper does not claim that
        these conditions hold for the synthetic regimes used here.
        Empirical verification of the dissipation inequality of T2 and
        the i.i.d.\ stationarity of T3 is queued as Experiment~1 of the
        companion programme.
\end{itemize}

\noindent We treat~\cite{paper5} as a stable preprint and not as a
peer-reviewed publication. Citations to it in the present paper should
be read in that spirit.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a parameterised and auditable design space for
neurosymbolic Digital Twin cybersecurity operators, instantiated by two
composable layers atop a previously validated operator~\cite{paper3}.
The Entropy Analysis Layer (EAL) promotes the entropy estimator, window
size, and stride to first-class deployment variables under a
parameter-aware stable-phase threshold; the Semantic Explainability
Layer (SEL) exposes the operator's reasoning to security operators
through three canonical SPARQL primitives on a CTI-aligned RDF
ontology. We characterised the resulting design space with a
$5\times 3\times 3$ factorial sensitivity study on synthetic regimes,
with a targeted single-scenario evaluation on a real-traffic DDoS
subset against a transparent statistical baseline, and with a small
SPARQL-scaling note in the $10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ triple range. The Pareto
front extracted from this design space spans multiple estimators,
evidencing that multi-estimator configurations are operationally
motivated under mixed risk profiles.

\medskip

The contribution is not a new detector competing on SOTA metrics. It is
a \emph{parameterised and auditable design space} that exposes the
configurability and queryability of neurosymbolic DT cyber-defense to
security operators and to regulators---complementing, not replacing,
the prior end-to-end validation of the underlying operator. The two
layers preserve the modular-update efficiency reported in prior
work~\cite{paper3} and expose an auditable design structure suitable as
technical evidence under emerging AI Act~\cite{aiact} and
NIS2~\cite{nis2} obligations on AI-enabled cybersecurity components.

\paragraph{Future work.} Future work develops the conditional
theoretical guarantees of EAL/SEL~\cite{paper5}, replaces the
transparent statistical baseline with a trained neural reference,
integrates the STIX/MISP export with a live CTI feed (with CVE binding
for zero-day events), and validates the operator-facing explanations
with security analysts in a human-centered XAI study. The SEL ontology
is also designed to ingest downstream supply-chain artefacts such as
SBOMs as recommended by ENISA~\cite{enisasbom}, an extension we identify
as part of the active research programme of the $\psiop$-Risk-DT line
at the University of Insubria.
